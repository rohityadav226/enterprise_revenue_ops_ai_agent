# Sales Execution Playbook

## 1. Purpose

This playbook provides practical guidance for moving qualified B2B opportunities through the NexusFlow sales process. It supplements the Sales Operating Policy and does not override approval requirements, CRM definitions, or signed customer terms.

## 2. Qualification checklist

Before advancing an opportunity beyond Qualification, the sales representative should document:

1. The business problem the prospect is trying to solve.
2. The expected operational or financial impact of solving it.
3. The business sponsor and relevant stakeholders.
4. The likely decision process and target decision date.
5. The initial solution fit and material gaps.
6. The next agreed action with an owner and date.

If essential information is unknown, record it as unknown and create a discovery action. Do not convert an assumption into a confirmed customer requirement.

## 3. Discovery guidance

A useful discovery conversation should establish the current process, operational pain points, affected teams, data sources, success measures, decision criteria, procurement steps, and implementation constraints.

The representative should summarize the customer's stated requirements and confirm the summary with the customer. Internal hypotheses should be labelled as hypotheses until validated.

## 4. Proposal guidance

A proposal should connect the customer's documented problem to the proposed solution, scope, outcomes, implementation assumptions, commercial terms, and success measures. The quote must use the approved price book and follow the Pricing and Discount Approval Policy.

Before moving an opportunity into Negotiation, record the proposal version, customer feedback, outstanding concerns, decision stakeholders, and expected decision date.

## 5. Negotiation and close planning

For an opportunity in Negotiation, maintain a close plan that lists each remaining commercial, technical, security, legal, procurement, and executive action. Each action should have an accountable owner and due date.

Do not mark an opportunity Closed Won solely because the customer has expressed verbal intent. Follow the approved contract and booking process.

## 6. Follow-up and inactivity guidance

The following thresholds are synthetic operational guidance for sales follow-up:

- After 14 calendar days without a completed customer-facing activity, the representative should review the next step and make an appropriate follow-up attempt.
- After 30 calendar days without a completed activity, the representative should document the reason for inactivity, confirm whether the opportunity is still active, and ask the manager for a review where the next step is unclear.
- At 60 calendar days without a completed activity, the manager should review the opportunity with the representative and agree a documented recovery, nurture, or close-out plan.

These inactivity thresholds are playbook guidance. They are not the same as the analytics system's at-risk flag. The current synthetic analytics rule identifies an opportunity as at risk when its age is at least 60 days or its expected close date is overdue. Opportunity age and time since last completed activity are different measures. Do not infer one from the other.

## 7. Recommended recovery plan

When an opportunity has stalled:

1. Review the last customer-confirmed objective and activity.
2. Identify the missing decision, stakeholder, proof point, or commercial approval.
3. Contact the customer with a specific and relevant next step rather than a generic check-in.
4. Reconfirm timing and decision process.
5. Update expected close date and stage only when supported by new evidence.
6. If there is no credible path forward, agree whether to nurture or close the opportunity with a documented reason.

## 8. Manager coaching questions

Managers should ask:

- What customer evidence supports the current stage?
- What is the next customer-confirmed action?
- Who owns the next action and by when?
- What decision or risk is preventing progress?
- Is the expected close date still credible?
- What support or approval does the representative need?

## 9. Customer communication standard

Communications should be accurate, relevant, professional, and specific to the customer's stated needs. Do not promise product capabilities, delivery dates, discounts, security terms, or contractual commitments that have not been approved.
