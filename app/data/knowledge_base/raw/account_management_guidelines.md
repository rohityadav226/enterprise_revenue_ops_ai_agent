# Account Management Guidelines

## 1. Purpose and scope

These guidelines describe the synthetic NexusFlow approach to account coverage, customer health reviews, renewals, and expansion planning. They are intended for account managers, customer success managers, sales teams, and Revenue Operations.

## 2. Account coverage model

Each active customer account should have a named account owner. Strategic accounts should also have an identified executive sponsor and a documented cross-functional coverage team.

The account owner coordinates the relationship and keeps account plans current. Customer Success owns adoption planning and product-use follow-up where that function is assigned. Sales and Finance remain responsible for approved commercial terms and contract records.

## 3. Account segments and review cadence

| Segment | Typical characteristics | Minimum structured business review |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic | High business impact, multiple stakeholder groups, material expansion potential, or complex deployment. | Monthly |
| Growth | Established use case with meaningful adoption or expansion opportunities. | Quarterly |
| Standard | More limited scope and stakeholder complexity, with standard support and renewal coverage. | At least twice per year |

These segments are relationship-coverage categories for the synthetic corpus. They must not be inferred from an account's revenue, employee count, or contract size unless a separate approved segmentation rule and the required data are available.

## 4. Account plan contents

An account plan should document:

- Customer objectives and expected outcomes.
- Products and use cases currently in scope.
- Executive sponsor, decision makers, champions, and operational contacts.
- Adoption goals and agreed success measures.
- Open support, delivery, security, or commercial risks.
- Renewal date, renewal owner, and renewal plan.
- Evidence-based expansion opportunities.
- Next scheduled customer review and actions.

## 5. Renewal planning timetable

Begin structured renewal planning at least:

| Account segment | Renewal planning starts |
|---|---|
| Strategic | 120 calendar days before contract expiry |
| Growth | 90 calendar days before contract expiry |
| Standard | 60 calendar days before contract expiry |

The account owner should confirm the renewal date against the executed contract or authoritative contract system. A CRM date that conflicts with the signed contract must be escalated for correction.

Renewal planning should review adoption, achieved outcomes, unresolved issues, stakeholder support, product fit, commercial changes, procurement requirements, and the customer's decision timeline.

## 6. Customer health indicators

The account team should review multiple evidence sources. Potential warning indicators include:

- A sustained decline in usage against the customer's agreed adoption baseline.
- An unresolved critical support issue.
- Loss of the primary champion or executive sponsor without a replacement.
- Repeatedly missed business reviews or agreed customer actions.
- Low adoption of a contracted capability that is central to the customer's stated outcome.
- Unresolved commercial or renewal concerns.

A single indicator should trigger investigation, not an unsupported declaration that the customer will churn. Record the evidence, customer impact, owner, and recovery action.

## 7. Expansion guidance

Expansion should be based on a validated customer need, demonstrated value, relevant stakeholder agreement, and an approved commercial proposal. Do not recommend additional products solely because they appear in a catalogue.

Potential expansion signals include a new business unit, additional approved use case, increased user population, or a customer-requested capability that fits the product roadmap. Validate the opportunity with the customer before creating a forecast assumption.

## 8. Account communications and data handling

Customer-facing commitments must be consistent with executed contracts and approved product, security, support, and pricing statements. Sensitive customer information must only be accessed by authorized personnel and stored in approved systems.

Account notes should distinguish customer-confirmed facts, internal hypotheses, and proposed actions. Do not present internal assumptions as customer statements.
